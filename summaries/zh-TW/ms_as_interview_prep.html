Cultural Fit

考你是否認同 Microsoft 的使命與「learn-it-all」文化。最忌空泛——要展現你對這個團隊的具體理解,以及為什麼這一步對你合理。內部轉組者尤其要把「為什麼是這個團隊」講清楚。

Mission alignment

Why this team? You're already at Microsoft — why move into this kind of weak-signal modeling work within Microsoft AI?

English answer

I've spent the last couple of years building evaluation pipelines and LLM-based query generation for a natural-language query system. The part of that work I kept gravitating toward wasn't the model — it was the measurement problem: how do you build a reliable reward signal when the ground truth is noisy, contested, or only partially observable?

That's exactly what this team does at a much larger scale. You're turning weak signals — page visits, dwell time, delayed conversions — into high-quality learning targets under partial feedback. I want to move here because the core challenge I find most interesting, learning from imperfect supervision, is the team's entire mandate, and it sits at web scale with direct, measurable business impact rather than benchmark scores.

Staying inside Microsoft also means I can be productive quickly — I already understand the engineering culture, the review processes, and how to ship responsibly here. I'm not changing companies; I'm moving toward the problem I'm most motivated to spend the next several years on.

中文總結:把動機落在「measurement／weak supervision」這個你真實的興趣點,而不是泛泛說想做 AI。明確連結:你做 query-generation eval 最著迷的是「ground truth 不可靠時怎麼建可靠的 reward 信號」——這正是這個團隊的全部工作。並打出內部轉組優勢:熟文化、上手快、不是換公司而是換到最想投入的問題。
Hook  面試前花十分鐘想一個你對這個團隊產品的具體觀察或好奇（例如 delayed-conversion 怎麼影響線上 A/B 判讀）,在結尾提問時拋出,展現你真的研究過。
Growth mindset · learn-it-all

Microsoft values a "learn-it-all" over "know-it-all" culture. Give an example of you living that.

English answer

SituationWhen I started studying RL for LLM post-training, I came in confident I understood evaluation — I'd been doing execution-based eval for SQL for a while.

ActionBut reading into RLVR, GRPO, and DPO, I realized my mental model of "reward" was too narrow. Instead of defending what I knew, I worked through the Databricks TAO paper line by line, re-implemented small pieces, and deliberately connected iterated offline RL back to my own query-generation setting with execution-based rewards — using my existing work as a sandbox to test the new ideas.

ResultThat reframing changed how I designed my next eval metric. It's also why I'm comfortable saying, in this interview, where the edges of my knowledge are — I'd rather surface a gap and close it than perform certainty.

中文總結:選一個你「以為自己懂、結果發現知識邊界」的真實例子（RL for post-training 很適合）。重點不是你最後學會了,而是你面對自己不足的反應:不防衛、主動拆解、用既有工作當沙盒驗證新概念。結尾用「我寧願承認 gap 並補上,也不假裝確定」收束,直接示範 growth mindset。

Behavioral

Driver for results · ownership

Tell me about a measurable impact you delivered where the ground truth or labels were unreliable.

English answer

SituationWhile evaluating our query-generation system on the BIRD benchmark, an entire dataset — the IPL cricket schema — was scoring near-zero execution accuracy. The obvious read was that our model was failing.

TaskI owned the eval pipeline, so I needed to know whether this was a real model regression or a measurement artifact before anyone acted on it.

ActionI traced individual failures and found two separate problems. First, some gold SQL answers were themselves wrong — the labels we were grading against had bugs. Second, a rewriting layer in our pipeline was mangling valid model-generated SQL — stripping GROUP BY clauses and promoting ORDER BY aliases — so correct queries were being marked wrong. I documented both, fixed the gold labels, and recommended strengthening the equivalence guard that compared result sets.

ResultExecution accuracy on that slice went from near-zero to reflecting true model performance, which meant every downstream decision built on that metric was now trustworthy. The deeper lesson: when a metric looks catastrophic, suspect the metric first. In a weak-label setting, label and pipeline integrity is the work.

中文總結:這是你最強的故事,直接打中 JD 的「noisy／weak labels」。結構:0 分看似模型爛 → 你 own eval pipeline,先懷疑量測 → 抓出兩個獨立問題(gold label 本身錯 + rewriter 破壞正確 SQL)→ 修正並強化 guard → 結果讓所有下游決策可信。金句:「metric 看起來災難時,先懷疑 metric」。把「label／pipeline 完整性本身就是工作」講出來,等於說你天生適合這個團隊。
Growth mindset · failure

Describe a time your initial approach didn't work and you had to pivot.

English answer

SituationFor schema lineage extraction, my first instinct was to lean on a standard string-overlap metric to score how well we recovered column-level lineage.

TaskI needed a metric that actually correlated with correctness, so the research claim would hold up.

ActionEarly results looked fine, but when I inspected cases by hand, the metric was rewarding outputs that were structurally wrong — it couldn't tell a shallow match from a real one. Rather than ship a number I didn't trust, I designed a new metric, SLiCE, that scored lineage in a way that reflected the structure we actually cared about, and validated it against roughly 1,700 hand-annotated examples.

ResultThat work became the basis of a NeurIPS workshop paper. The pivot cost me time up front, but a metric you can't trust quietly corrupts every experiment downstream — so I'd make the same call again.

中文總結:失敗故事但展現判斷力:第一版用現成 metric,手動檢查發現它在獎勵「結構錯誤但表面像」的輸出 → 不願交出自己不信的數字 → 自建 SLiCE,用 ~1700 標註驗證 → 成為 NeurIPS workshop paper。結尾把「不可信的 metric 會悄悄汙染所有下游實驗」點出來,跟前一題呼應,強化你對 evaluation 的執著。
注意  這題與上題不要用同一個故事。上題是「debug 別人的錯」,這題是「推翻自己的選擇」——後者才是 growth mindset 真正要的「對自己」的反思。
Collaboration · influence without authority

Tell me about a time you had to influence another team that didn't report to you.

English answer

SituationThe rewriting layer that was corrupting valid SQL was owned by a different part of the pipeline, not my code.

ActionI didn't just file a bug. I put together a tight write-up with concrete failing cases — input SQL, what the rewriter produced, and why the result set comparison then failed — and proposed a specific fix to the equivalence guard rather than asking them to "look into it." I framed it around shared cost: every bad rewrite was inflating their component's error attribution too.

ResultBecause the evidence was specific and the ask was concrete, the fix was prioritized instead of getting lost. The general lesson I took: cross-team influence is mostly about reducing the other team's effort to say yes — bring the diagnosis and a proposed fix, not just a complaint.

中文總結:展現跨團隊影響力。重點:不只報 bug,而是給出具體 failing cases + 直接提出修法,並用「共同成本」框架(壞 rewrite 也在膨脹他們元件的錯誤歸因)。金句:跨團隊影響力的本質是「降低對方說 yes 的成本」。這對應 JD 強調的 cross-functional coordination。

Technical (non-coding)

考 ML 基礎與你能否在架構／實作層解釋「為什麼這樣做」。對這個團隊,重點集中在 weak/delayed labels、calibration、offline vs online evaluation。能把概念連回你做過的 execution-based reward 與 eval pipeline 是加分關鍵。

Weak supervision · core to JD

In ad conversion modeling, true outcomes are often delayed or never observed. How would you build training signals under that constraint?

English answer

I'd separate two failure modes: label delay and label absence, because they need different treatments.

For delayed conversions, the danger is that a recent window looks negative simply because the conversion hasn't landed yet. So I wouldn't naively treat "no conversion observed" as a hard negative. Approaches like delayed-feedback modeling — explicitly modeling the time-to-conversion distribution and reweighting or correcting recent labels — keep the model from learning a biased picture of fresh traffic. I'd also be explicit about the attribution window as a modeling choice, not a fixed given.

For unobservable outcomes, I'd lean on proxy and multi-task signals — dwell time, downstream engagement events — as auxiliary objectives that correlate with the true target, while being careful that the proxy doesn't quietly become the objective. This is where my own work transfers directly: in my query-generation work I used execution-based rewards as a proxy for correctness — does the query return the right result set — and the constant risk was a query that's right for the wrong reasons. The discipline is the same: validate that the proxy actually tracks the target, on a trusted held-out slice, before you trust it at scale.

中文總結:先拆兩種情況——delay 與 absence,展現結構化思考。Delay:不要把「還沒看到轉換」當硬負例,用 delayed-feedback model 修正近期標籤;把 attribution window 當成建模選擇。Absence:用 proxy／multi-task 信號(dwell time、下游互動),但警惕 proxy 變成目標本身。再橋接你的 execution-based reward 經驗(「對的結果但錯的理由」風險),點出共同紀律:在可信的 held-out 上驗證 proxy 真的追蹤目標。
Calibration

Why does calibration matter for a model that feeds ad ranking and pricing, and how would you check it?

English answer

In ranking, you sometimes only need the order to be right. But the moment predicted probabilities feed pricing or bidding, the absolute value matters — if predicted conversion rates are systematically inflated, you mis-price every auction, and the error compounds across the marketplace. So calibration isn't cosmetic here; it's directly tied to advertiser ROI and revenue.

To check it, I'd start with a reliability diagram — bucket predictions and compare predicted vs observed rates — plus a summary like expected calibration error. Crucially I'd check calibration per segment, not just globally: a model can be well-calibrated on average while being badly off for new advertisers or low-volume slices, which is exactly where business risk hides. For correction, temperature scaling or isotonic regression on a held-out set are standard, but I'd re-check calibration drift over time, since marketplace dynamics shift.

中文總結:關鍵區分:ranking 有時只需排序對,但一旦預測機率餵給 pricing／bidding,絕對值就重要——系統性高估會錯估每場競價,誤差在市場中複利累積,直接影響廣告主 ROI。檢查:reliability diagram + ECE,並且要分 segment 檢查(整體準但對新廣告主／低量切片可能很偏,風險就藏在那)。校正:temperature scaling／isotonic regression,並追蹤 calibration drift。
Offline ↔ online gap

Your model improves on offline metrics but the online A/B test is flat. How do you diagnose it?

English answer

I'd treat this as a chain and check each link. First, the metric link: is the offline metric actually a faithful proxy for the online objective? A gain on an offline surrogate that doesn't track the business metric is the most common culprit — I've been burned by exactly this in eval work, where a metric improved while real correctness didn't.

Second, distribution shift: offline data is historical; online traffic may differ, and there can be feedback loops where the model changes the very distribution it's scored on. Third, the experiment itself: is it powered enough to detect the effect size? Is the randomization unit right? Are guardrail metrics masking a mixed result — some segments up, others down, netting to flat? I'd segment the online result before concluding the model "did nothing." Often "flat overall" is really "a win and a loss cancelling out," and that's a design insight, not a failure.

中文總結:當成一條鏈逐環檢查。① metric link:offline metric 是否忠實代理 online 目標?——最常見元兇,你在 eval 工作就被這個坑過。② distribution shift:offline 是歷史資料,且可能有 feedback loop。③ 實驗本身:power 夠嗎?randomization unit 對嗎?guardrail 是否遮住了混合結果(有些 segment 升、有些降,淨值打平)?先分 segment 再下結論——「整體打平」常常是「一勝一負互相抵銷」,那是設計洞見不是失敗。

ML System Design

出聲走完從資料蒐集到 production 部署的全鏈路,每一步講 trade-off。對這個 level,他們看你能否獨立 drive 一個含資料、label 建構、訓練、offline/online eval、上線監控的端到端系統。框架比細節重要。

End-to-end · estimated conversion

Design an estimated-conversion (pCVR) model that feeds ad ranking and bidding at web scale.

English answer — 用這個結構出聲走一遍

1 · ClarifyFirst I'd pin down the objective and constraints: are we optimizing for ranking order, or do downstream pricing decisions need calibrated absolute probabilities? What's the latency budget at serving, and what's the conversion-delay horizon? These answers change the whole design.

2 · Data & labelsThis is the hard part and where I'd spend the most time. Positives are conversions, but with a delay horizon, so I'd define an attribution window and use delayed-feedback handling so recent traffic isn't mislabeled negative. I'd build weak/proxy labels (engagement, dwell) as auxiliary targets. I'd be explicit about label-quality checks — in my experience the silent killer is corrupted labels, not model capacity.

3 · ModelA transformer-based model over user history and ad/content representations, with multi-task heads: the main pCVR head plus auxiliary proxy heads that regularize the representation. I'd justify multi-task by the sparsity of the true signal.

4 · EvaluationOffline: AUC/PR for ranking and calibration metrics (reliability, ECE) per segment, since pricing needs calibration. Online: A/B with a clear randomization unit, primary business metric plus guardrails, powered for the expected effect size. I'd explicitly plan for the offline-online gap.

5 · Serving & monitoringCalibration drift monitoring, delayed-label backfill, and retraining cadence tied to marketplace shift. I'd close with the top risk: proxy objectives drifting away from true value over time.

中文總結:用五步框架出聲走:① Clarify——先問目標(只需排序 vs 需校準絕對值)、延遲時長、serving latency,這些決定整個設計。② Data & labels——花最多時間在這:定 attribution window、用 delayed-feedback 處理,建 proxy labels,強調 label 品質檢查(你的招牌)。③ Model——transformer + multi-task heads,用真實信號稀疏性來 justify multi-task。④ Evaluation——offline 要 ranking + 分 segment 的 calibration;online A/B 講清 randomization unit、guardrail、power;預先規劃 offline-online gap。⑤ Serving & monitoring——calibration drift、delayed-label backfill、retrain 節奏。收尾點最大風險:proxy 隨時間偏離真實價值。
關鍵  這個 level 的 system design 不要急著畫架構。前 2 分鐘的 clarifying questions 與「我會在 label 建構花最多時間」這句話,本身就是強信號——它證明你知道這個團隊真正難的地方在哪。

  • Design a system to detect and correct label noise in a continuously growing training set.
  • Design a multi-task model serving both click and conversion prediction — how do you share vs separate?
  • How would you build a feature pipeline robust to delayed and out-of-order event streams?
  • Design A/B experimentation for a model whose effect is delayed by days (delayed conversions).

Case Study

給你一個產品情境,要你把模糊問題拆成可解的 ML 問題,並連回商業價值。評分看:問題框定、量測定義、trade-off,以及你有沒有 customer/business 視角而不只談技術。

Metric framing · ambiguous problem

A new ad-signal feature ships. Advertisers complain ROI "feels worse," but your offline metrics are up. How do you investigate?

English answer

I'd resist picking a side immediately — "the metric is right" or "the advertisers are right" are both premature. I'd frame it as: where could the offline metric and the advertiser's lived ROI diverge?

First I'd define what "ROI feels worse" actually means — is it spend efficiency, conversion volume, conversion quality, or attribution timing? Vague complaints usually encode a real signal in a specific segment. Then I'd check whether the offline metric is measuring the same thing advertisers feel: a model can improve average pCVR AUC while degrading calibration for a specific advertiser tier, so they're systematically over- or under-bid. I'd segment by advertiser size, vertical, and conversion delay.

I'd also check for a delayed-conversion artifact: if the new feature shifted timing, recent ROI looks worse simply because conversions haven't landed yet — a measurement illusion, not a real loss. The output isn't "who's right"; it's a decomposition that tells us whether to fix the model, fix the metric, or fix how we communicate attribution timing to advertisers.

中文總結:不要急著選邊(「metric 對」或「廣告主對」都太早)。框定成:offline metric 與廣告主實際 ROI 可能在哪裡分歧?① 先定義「ROI feels worse」到底指什麼(花費效率?轉換量?轉換品質?歸因時間?)——含糊抱怨通常編碼了某個 segment 的真信號。② 檢查 metric 是否量到廣告主感受的同一件事(整體 AUC 升但某層廣告主 calibration 退化 → 被系統性錯估出價);分 advertiser size／vertical／delay 切。③ 檢查 delayed-conversion 假象:新功能若改變了轉換時間,近期 ROI 變差只是轉換還沒落地的錯覺。產出不是「誰對」,而是一個分解,告訴我們該修模型、修 metric、還是修跟廣告主溝通歸因時間的方式。
